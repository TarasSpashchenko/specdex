# Task H05

**Question:** Can a kid get extra minutes by moving the phone's date and time forward, even if they have no connection and then turn the phone off and on?

**Gold answer:** Play time uses monotonic elapsed-time deltas, and trusted time never runs backward or advances because of a manual clock change. However, after a reboot with no trusted anchor the device clock is accepted, so changing it offline and rebooting can grant play time; that residual risk is recorded as ACR-J and CR-1 residual (c).

- _bmad-output/planning-artifacts/architecture/architecture-tamagai-2026-10-03/ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md:521-542 — AD-18 specifies the time source and explicitly qualifies the clock-change guarantee after reboot.

**Labeller notes:** Prompt avoids clock-change, trusted-anchor, monotonic and reboot terminology; the residual risk is essential.

## Answer A

Yes, in one narrow case. Play time is counted with the phone's monotonic `elapsedRealtime` clock, and "trusted now" is the last trusted anchor plus elapsed time. A manual clock change doesn't advance trusted time, so moving the date forward alone gives no extra minutes. The anchors are the network time clock, the server's HTTPS `Date` header, and the device clock when it agrees with the estimate. A reboot resets the monotonic clock. If there is also no usable anchor because the phone is offline, the spec says the device clock is accepted. So changing the clock offline and then rebooting can grant play time. The architecture records this as a known residual risk (ACR-J, CR-1 residual (c)) and does not close it. The PRD's requirement that a time change never grants play time (tested at ±24 hours) is only fully met while a trusted anchor exists.

## Answer B

Yes, in that one case. Play time is counted with a monotonic clock, and normally a manual date or time change doesn't grant any. But if the phone is offline and has no usable trusted time anchor, a reboot makes the device clock the accepted time. A clock change followed by that reboot can then grant play time. The specs record this as a known residual risk (ACR-J, CR-1 residual (c)) and mark it `[ASSUMPTION: CQ-22]`. FR-59 says the behavior is tested at ±24 hours, including the reboot case.

## Answer C

Yes, if the phone restarts offline without a usable trusted time anchor, moving its date and time forward can grant extra play time. The app then accepts the phone’s clock; while a trusted anchor remains available, clock changes cannot grant time. The specifications explicitly acknowledge this loophole as residual risk ACR-J/CR-1, under assumption CQ-22.
