## Answer B

Improve how the purchase is explained first. The specs set a switch trigger: if refunds go above 10%, fix the demo and the listing before touching the price. The CFO red-team digest gives the reasoning: a refund rate that high means the tier disclosure is failing, so the problem is what buyers understand before they pay. The planning target is refunds under 5%, with a 5–8% budget for the 14-day refund window. Price changes are reserved for other triggers, such as demo-to-unlock conversion under 2% over the first 5,000 installs, or non-buyer costs above 10% of net. Those would move the app to paid-upfront.

## Answer C

Improve how the purchase is explained first. The addendum's switch-trigger table says that when refunds go above 10%, the response is "Fix the demo and the listing before touching the price." The "demo" and "listing" are the pre-purchase explanation, so price comes last. The addendum also says price can't be properly tested in early access. Detecting a 20% relative difference in conversion needs about 8,500 installs per price. Price is therefore set from anchors and revisited after launch. The trigger is marked as a reasoned figure (`[RF]`), not a measured one.
